\begin{abstract}
 K-12 schools are increasingly targeted by cyber threats, yet operate with limited IT staff, no dedicated security budget, and no access to enterprise-grade tools. Existing solutions rely on RBAC policies, which enforce access based on predefined roles but cannot detect anomalous behavior within those roles. This paper focuses on PirateShield, a zero-cost cybersecurity framework for K-12 schools. It specifically examines its Access-Control layer, which addresses this gap by extending RBAC with behavioral monitoring - tracking session patterns, resource access habits, and time-based behavior - and Access Velocity scoring, which measures the rate at which a user is performing actions. Together, these layers enable PirateShield to flag threats that role-based policies alone would miss, making enterprise-level threat detection accessible to schools with no security infrastructure.

{\textbf{Keywords -}} PirateShield, access control, RBAC, behavioral monitoring, K-12
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Many K-12 schools depend heavily on third-party platforms such as Canvas, Google Classroom, Infinite Campus, PowerSchool, etc. (EdTech). Research has found that 89\% of the technologies they studied violated students' privacy through surveillance and sharing data with third parties for advertising purposes. Furthermore, the U.S. Government Accountability Office found 99 student data breaches affecting hundreds of school districts over a 4-year period. [1] To combat this issue, our goal is to release PirateShield, a multi-layer cybersecurity system for K-12 schools that observes identity, device, network, and access-behavior over time. Access-control systems serve as the first line of defense for protecting sensitive data and resources. In PirateShield, role-based policies form this foundation, as they enforce predefined access rules to flag when users act outside their expected permissions. That said, RBAC has a well-known limitation. Once permissions are assigned, nothing is stopping a user from misusing them. This applies to both insider threats (legitimate users abusing their access) and external attackers (who steal credentials to impersonate someone). PirateShield will take this into account by layering behavioral monitoring and access velocity on top of RBAC, enabling it to flag anomalies when a user's actions deviate from expectations for their role.

\subsection{How to write Mathematics}

\LaTeX{} is great at typesetting mathematics. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables with $\text{E}[X_i] = \mu$ and $\text{Var}[X_i] = \sigma^2 < \infty$, and let
\[S_n = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n}{n}
      = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}^{n} X_i\]
denote their mean. Then as $n$ approaches infinity, the random variables $\sqrt{n}(S_n - \mu)$ converge in distribution to a normal $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.


\subsection{How to change the margins and paper size}

Usually the template you're using will have the page margins and paper size set correctly for that use-case.
